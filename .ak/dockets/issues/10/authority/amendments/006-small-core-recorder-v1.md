# Authority amendment 006: small-core Recorder v1

This amendment adopts `owner-synthesis-006` and expressly supersedes amendment 004's generic cold forensic stream and amendment 005's generic raw-session admission/retention. Prior amendments, Receipts, reviews, judgments, and manifests remain byte-identical historical artifacts; this forward correction is the governing v1 law where they conflict.

## 1. No cold or generic raw subsystem in v1

Recorder v1 has no cold root, cold locator, raw admission reasons, raw admission ranges, compression/chunking format, retention policy, or second storage temperature. Those mechanisms are not deferred options inside v1; they are absent.

## 2. Ordered small-core lifecycle and fail-closed outcomes

Successful Recorder completion is one ordered invariant:

1. capture raw private scratch as needed;
2. credential-scan/redact, extract the package-accepted typed Receipt/audit acceptance when one exists, and stage the complete small core;
3. perform required raw-scratch cleanup;
4. atomically promote the staged small core.

Cleanup failure or promotion failure is Recorder infrastructure failure and leaves no apparently complete docket. A final manifest exists only after successful promotion. That manifest records the child outcome and successful Recorder completion. When both child and Recorder fail, child diagnostics are preserved; Recorder failure has outward precedence. The Recorder never synthesizes a Receipt.

## 3. No generic raw objects or ranges

Recorder admits no generic raw session object, tool-event object, or raw range. When a session/event lifecycle is itself irreducible proof, that proof is created through the affected seam as one canonical fixture/probe artifact, then deleted or graduated exactly once under repository `CLAUDE.md` probe law, with no Recorder-held duplicate. The admitted result is a whole, byte-sealed ordinary exhibit supplied to the core—not a second permanent raw copy under Recorder retention.

## 4. Artifact identity

Already-committed bytes are identified only by mechanically verified repository identity, full commit SHA, repository-relative path, Git blob OID, and SHA-256 of the exact bytes. Each genuinely new Receipt, decision artifact, external input, or exhibit is stored exactly once at its canonical path with its exact-byte digest.

Embedded session/tool echoes are not artifacts. Generated bytes are not a pending Git reference and must not claim a future containing commit. The caller owns commit creation. Downstream invocations and closure bind the actual full target HEAD where durable Git resolvability is required. No mandatory follow-up self-seal exists merely to restate what Git already records.

## 5. Mandatory identities; omission is not compliance

Every authority/task/input byte and every claim-supporting exhibit must use one of the two exact identities in §4: a mechanically verified Git reference, or one stored byte-sealed artifact. Omission or disposition cannot satisfy this requirement. Reference verification failure, admission failure, redaction failure, cleanup failure, or promotion failure follows the same Recorder-infrastructure-failure, atomicity, and no-Receipt-synthesis law.

## 6. Final-branch reconstruction

The final mergeable issue-10 branch must be reconstructed from the default branch, or by an equivalent Git-object-removing construction—not by forward deletion of large blobs from the oversized feature history. Preserve exact small historical Receipts, manifests, authority, reviews, adjudications, plans, and migration dispositions. Existing manifests that cite now-omitted payloads remain byte-identical and receive separate forward supersession/disposition records; they are not rewritten to look current. The approximately 398 MiB session/tool-event blobs are execution exhaust and are excluded from the reconstructed claim chain after capacity measurement and accepted Receipts carry the relevant claims.

## 7. Measurable reconstruction evidence

Later authorized Plan/Apply evidence must report the reconstructed issue-10 claim-chain tree's exact logical-byte size and mechanically prove that the reconstructed ancestry/inventory contains no generic session or tool-event payload. “A few MiB” remains the owner objective for ordinary tracked claim-chain material; it is not a numeric retention engine inside Recorder.

## Ownership boundaries

- **Caller** owns commit creation and full-HEAD binding where required.
- **Affected seam** owns fixture/probe creation, deletion, and exactly-once graduation under repository law.
- **Later authorized Plan/Apply** owns final-branch reconstruction and its exact size plus ancestry/inventory proof.
- **Recorder** owns only the ordered small-core lifecycle and fail-closed outcome semantics above.

## Scope exclusion

This amendment authorizes no Recorder implementation, test-fixture creation, archived-payload inspection, cold-store replacement, Git LFS, archive service, summarizer, model admission role, database, catalog, daemon, or automatic Git mutation.
